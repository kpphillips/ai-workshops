Looking things up

A model can answer from its weights, from a retrieved page, from a long prompt, or from a tool that opens a file. This workshop runs all four against one small handbook so you can see what each one actually does.

1. What retrieval-augmented generation is, and where a fixed window cuts

In 2020 Lewis and colleagues defined retrieval-augmented generation as a generator with two memories. The parametric memory is the generator's weights. In their system that generator was BART-large, which they describe as a seq2seq Transformer with 400 million parameters. The non-parametric memory was a dense index of Wikipedia, built from the December 2018 dump and split into disjoint 100-word chunks, 21 million of them, retrieved by a pretrained Dense Passage Retriever. Quoted arXiv:2005.11401

They trained two ways of using the retrieved chunk. RAG-Sequence conditions every token of the answer on the same retrieved chunk. RAG-Token may use a different chunk for each token. Both marginalize over a top-k list. They kept the document encoder fixed and fine-tuned the query encoder and BART. Quoted

On the Natural Questions open-domain test set they report exact-match of 44.5 for RAG-Sequence and 44.1 for RAG-Token, against 41.5 for the DPR extractive pipeline and 34.5 for a closed-book T5 with 11 billion parameters. The abstract says the same models set the state of the art on three open-domain question sets: Natural Questions, WebQuestions, and CuratedTrec. Those are 2020 numbers on those sets. They are not a ranking of later systems. Quoted

The introduction names three limits of a weights-only model: it cannot easily expand or revise its memory, it cannot straightforwardly show where an answer came from, and it may produce hallucinations. In an index-swap test they built a second index from the December 2016 Wikipedia dump and asked “Who is {position}?” about 82 world leaders who had changed by December 2018. RAG answered 70% correctly with the 2016 index on the 2016 leaders and 68% with the 2018 index on the 2018 leaders. The 2018 index on the 2016 leaders scored 12%, and the 2016 index on the 2018 leaders scored 4%. Quoted

A different cut, which that paper does not measure Their index unit was a 100-word Wikipedia chunk. A handbook procedure is not a Wikipedia paragraph. 2. Put the whole handbook in the prompt

Retrieval exists partly because the prompt used to be short. A long context window is a different design: paste the pages, and do not retrieve. Pasting has a budget, in tokens and in money. It also has a use question, which is not the same as the budget question.

Liu and colleagues, in 2023, studied multi-document question answering and a synthetic key-value task. They report that, on the models they tested, accuracy was often highest when the relevant text sat at the beginning or the end of the prompt, and lower when it sat in the middle. The models were MPT-30B-Instruct, LongChat-13B (16K), GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Claude-1.3. On their multi-document task, GPT-3.5-Turbo with the relevant document in the middle scored below its own closed-book figure of 56.1%. In a separate Natural Questions case study, moving from 20 retrieved documents to 50 improved GPT-3.5-Turbo by about 1.5 points and Claude-1.3 by about 1 point, which they describe with a tilde. Quoted arXiv:2307.03172

That paper does not measure models from 2026, and this page does not repeat the experiment. The lab only does the budget.

3. Word scores, teaching vectors, and rank fusion

The 2020 RAG system retrieved with a dense bi-encoder. In the same paper they also swapped that retriever for BM25 and used the BM25 scores as the retrieval logits. They report that BM25 performed best on FEVER in that comparison, and that the learned retriever improved the other tasks they measured. Quoted A word score is the method that lights up when the query contains a rare string that also appears on the page. This module runs a word score, a hand-built vector score, and one merge of the two lists, on eight fictional pages. It is not a rerun of their table.

BM25 scores a page by summing, over the query words that appear on it, a product of an inverse-document-frequency term and a saturated term frequency, with a penalty for page length. Robertson and Zaragoza (2009) write that the model does not say how to set the saturation parameter k1 and the length parameter b, and that experiments suggest values such as 0.5 < b < 0.8 and 1.2 < k1 < 2 are reasonably good on many collections, while the best values depend on the collection. Quoted The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond

This page sets k1 = 1.2 and b = 0.75. b sits inside the range they report. k1 sits on the edge of the strict range they wrote. Both are a choice. Convention The IDF term is ln(1 + (N − df + 0.5) / (df + 0.5)), where N is the number of pages and df is how many pages contain the word. That expression is also a choice. Convention Tokens are lowercase words and dotted identifiers. There is no stemmer, so "visitor" does not match "visitors" unless the page uses that form. A short stop list is dropped. Convention

The second ranker is not an embedding model. Each page and each fixed question has a hand-built vector over a few concepts (remote, credential, wireless, guest, and so on). The score is cosine similarity. Empty vectors are omitted. These vectors exist so a paraphrase can outrank a page that shares no words. They will miss an identifier they have no dimension for. Illustrative

The third list is reciprocal rank fusion. Cormack, Clarke, and Buettcher (SIGIR 2009) define the score of a document as the sum, over rankings, of 1 / (k + rank), with k = 60 fixed in a pilot and not changed afterward. On the TREC runs they combined, they report that this beat Condorcet fusion and, with one exception where the best run was manual, the best single run. Quoted their SIGIR paper This page uses k = 60 and 1-based ranks. A page missing from a list adds nothing from that list. Equal scores keep the earlier page in the handbook. Convention Their TREC result is not a result on this handbook.

4. Let the model search with tools

A retriever returns a list in one shot. A tool loop can grep, read, and grep again. Yao and colleagues (2022) describe ReAct as interleaving a reasoning trace with actions that read an external source. On HotpotQA and FEVER they used a Wikipedia API. On ALFWorld and WebShop they report absolute success-rate gains of 34 and 10 points against the imitation and reinforcement-learning methods in that paper, using one or two in-context examples. Quoted arXiv:2210.03629

Those are their tasks. The stepper here is a scripted trace on the fictional handbook. It is not a model log, and it is not a ReAct run. Illustrative Watch the context grow, and watch a first read that shares a word with the question and does not hold the answer.

5. Fine-tuning and retrieval change different things

Fine-tuning continues training and changes weights. Retrieval leaves the weights alone and adds text at question time. They are not two scores on one axis, and a paper that compares them has a setup.

Ovadia, Brief, Mishaeli, and Elisha (2023) compared unsupervised fine-tuning, meaning continued training on the raw text, with RAG. Their abstract says RAG consistently outperformed that fine-tuning, both for knowledge the models had seen in pretraining and for new knowledge, and that the models struggled to learn new facts from unsupervised fine-tuning alone. The conclusion says they found RAG the more reliable choice for knowledge injection in that work. Quoted arXiv:2312.05934

The same paper's limitations and future-work sections bound that sentence. They used unsupervised training, not instruction-tuning and not reinforcement learning. They used three models. Every knowledge base was from Wikipedia. The number of retrieved passages, K, was unstable across tasks, and they call that instability a practical downside of RAG. They say generalization to other models should be tested, and that hyperparameters matter. Quoted This page did not fine-tune a model and did not repeat their tests.

Lewis and colleagues, separately, showed that replacing the non-parametric index updates what their 2020 system can answer. That is a property of an index you can rebuild. It is not a property of every file a tool might read, and it says nothing about a fine-tune whose job is the shape of a reply rather than a fact.

6. Count hits on five questions

Manning, Raghavan, and Schütze define recall as the fraction of relevant documents that the system retrieved. Recall at k uses only the top k. With one relevant page, recall at k is 1 if that page is in the top k and 0 otherwise. Quoted Introduction to Information Retrieval, the unranked-evaluation section; the cutoff at k is the ranked variant of that ratio.

Five questions cannot settle a method. The tally is a teaching count on this handbook. The banner on the result says so. Two columns can share a count and miss different questions. Read the rows.
